The problem

Seeing is no longer believing

AI fakes pass every eye test

AI now produces photos and video that nobody can tell from real ones. Chasing the fakes is a losing game — proving the real ones are real is the durable answer.

Platforms erase the evidence

Every social platform recompresses what you upload and strips out anything attached to the file. Whatever certificate travelled with it is gone exactly where media actually circulates.

Trust has a price tag

Fake media drives hundreds of billions in fraud every year — insurance claims, identity checks, legal evidence — and new laws now require media to carry proof of where it came from.

Step by step

How it works

1 · Capture

Photo or video, from the TrustCam app — saved to your gallery like any camera. Everything that follows happens on the phone, even offline.

2 · Invisible mark

An invisible mark is woven into the image itself. Compressing, resizing, re-uploading — it survives what social platforms do to files.

3 · Sealed by the chip

The file's digital fingerprint is signed by a key that lives inside your phone's security chip and physically cannot leave it — no malware, no hack can steal it.

4 · Anyone verifies

Drop any copy of the file on the public verifier: who recorded it, with which phone, when — and whether that exact copy is untouched.

Double protection

Two layers, because one is never enough

The seal proves a file is exactly as recorded, but doesn't survive social media. The invisible mark survives social media, but can't prove a file is untouched. TrustCam ships both — each covers the other's blind spot.

Layer 1 — The seal

A digital signature made inside your phone's security chip. If a file is the exact one that was recorded, the seal proves it — change a single pixel and the check fails. Full details in the paper.

Layer 2 — The invisible mark

A mark hidden in the image itself, built on Meta's VideoSeal research. When platforms strip everything else, the pixels still testify.

Why now

Why now

2023–24

First cameras that sign photos ship (Leica, Sony). The industry agrees on a common standard, backed by 6,000+ organizations.

2025

Google Pixel signs photos by default. Proof-of-origin reaches consumer phones — photos only, flagship only.

Aug 2026

EU law now requires AI-generated content to be labeled. Proving what's real is the complement every publisher will need.

Jan 2028

California will require new cameras and phones to embed proof-of-origin by default. The window for independent verification is now.

Video — the hardest and most valuable case — is still unclaimed territory: no phone on the market seals video with a mark that survives social media. TrustCam does.

Transparency

Honest by design

Three verdicts, no gray areas

Green — the exact file, seal intact: authentic and untouched. Amber — the mark was found: we know where it comes from, not that it's unchanged. Red — no proof. We never call a modified copy authentic. Full case table.

What a proof means

“This exact file was recorded by this specific phone, on this account, at this time — and is unchanged since.” Nothing is taken on trust: the proof is re-checked from scratch every single time you verify.

What it doesn't

No camera can prove the scene was real — filming a screen produces a genuine recording of a fake. We say precisely what we can prove and nothing more. In this market, overclaiming is fatal; our limits are public.
