TrustCam: capture-time provenance for photos and video

Project description and technical documentation.

Abstract

As generative models make fabricated photos and videos indistinguishable from real captures, "detecting fakes" is a losing race. TrustCam takes the complementary approach: proving authentic media authentic at the moment of capture. The mobile app embeds a robust invisible watermark (Meta VideoSeal) into the pixels on the device, then hashes the final file and signs it with an ECDSA P-256 key generated inside the phone's secure hardware (StrongBox / TEE), whose attestation chain binds the key to hardware. The signed proof travels inside the file itself (a metadata trailer) — there are no accounts and no servers. A fully static, client-side verifier lets anyone confirm who recorded a file, when, and that it is byte-for-byte unmodified — and, because the watermark survives social-media re-encoding, provenance can be recovered even after platforms strip metadata (validated in our feasibility experiments). The watermark carries a random 128-bit capture id (BCH-protected): given any surviving copy, the verifier recovers the id, resolves it to the original capture on file, and shows the two side by side — so an edited copy can be told from a merely re-shared one even when the cryptographic seal is long gone.

The roadmap adds the cloud registry the capture id points into (devices register at setup, sealed originals upload at capture) and per-segment rolling signatures carried in the video bitstream (ONVIF Media Signing), validated on consumer hardware in our feasibility tests.

Claims and non-claims

A valid TrustCam proof asserts: this exact file was produced by this specific device, at this (device-claimed) time, and has not been modified since. It deliberately does not assert that the depicted scene is real — no capture-time cryptography can: a trusted camera filming a high-resolution display signs what its sensor sees (the "analog hole"). Likewise, a watermark-only match is an origin claim, not authentication: the side-by-side comparison with the verified original is evidence for human judgement, but only the signed trailer is cryptographic proof. Systems that overclaim here have failed publicly; we scope claims to what the cryptography actually proves.
