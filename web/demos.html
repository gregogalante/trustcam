Photo — through WhatsApp

Captured with the app on a Motorola moto g75, shared on WhatsApp, saved back from the receiving phone. WhatsApp cut the file to an eighth and stripped everything attached — the mark in the pixels brought the original back.

re-encoded the JPEG, halved each side (4× fewer pixels, 8× smaller file), stripped the proof

Photo — reworked by AI

The same photo fed to ChatGPT for an edit. The AI repaints every pixel — and the invisible mark dies with them. The reworked copy gets no proof at all: it cannot borrow the original's identity. That is the fail-safe working as designed.

Recognition numbers

What the AI did
regenerated the whole image (same scene, altered details — the road signs changed)
Mark recovery
none — 48 of 128 payload bits flipped (statistically random), decode confidence 60%, below every threshold
Verdict
No proof found — the AI copy carries no TrustCam identity, and is never called authentic

Video — through WhatsApp and YouTube

Same capture, two platforms. WhatsApp crushed it to a quarter of the pixels at an eighth of the bitrate; YouTube Shorts re-encoded it to a different codec entirely (AV1). The mark survived both.

Recognition numbers

WhatsApp copy
75% of the pixels gone, bitrate cut 8× — mark signal 89%, capture id recovered
YouTube Short
re-encoded to AV1 (a codec that didn't touch the original) — mark signal 91%, capture id recovered
Verdict
both copies: Origin traced — verified original on file, shown side by side

The YouTube copy is live: youtube.com/shorts/hG7Fs3LzxeE. Mark signal is how reliably the invisible mark was decoded — a measure of the surviving watermark, not of authenticity: the proof of what the capture looked like is the verified original itself.
