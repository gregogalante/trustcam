Where the limit is: Instagram's square crop

The same photo posted as a 1:1 square loses 25% of the frame to Instagram's crop — beyond what the mark's error correction can absorb, even after the verifier restores the original framing. The verdict is honestly No proof found rather than a guess. Posts that keep the frame (or the standard 4:5 portrait crop) verify fine. Download the square copy and see for yourself.

Photo — with a trusted timestamp

Since app 1.4.0 the proof carries an RFC 3161 token: at capture the app sends the file's SHA-256 — never the image — to a public timestamp authority, whose signed clock lands in the proof. The capture time is no longer just the phone's word: a third party attests the file existed no later than that moment. Sealed offline? The token is simply absent and the time stays honestly device-claimed.

Photo — reworked by AI

The same photo fed to ChatGPT for an edit. The AI repaints every pixel — and the invisible mark dies with them. The reworked copy gets no proof at all: it cannot borrow the original's identity. That is the fail-safe working as designed.

Recognition numbers

What the AI did
regenerated the whole image (same scene, altered details — the road signs changed)
Mark recovery
none — 48 of 128 payload bits flipped (statistically random), decode confidence 60%, below every threshold
Verdict
No proof found — the AI copy carries no TrustCam identity, and is never called authentic

Video — through WhatsApp, YouTube and Instagram

Same capture, three platforms. WhatsApp crushed it to a quarter of the pixels at an eighth of the bitrate; YouTube Shorts re-encoded it to a different codec entirely (AV1); Instagram re-encoded it at 720p. The mark survived all three.

YouTube Short

Instagram copy
re-encoded at 720p — mark signal 99%, capture id recovered
Verdict
all three copies: Origin traced — verified original on file, shown side by side

The YouTube copy is live: youtube.com/shorts/hG7Fs3LzxeE. Mark signal is how reliably the invisible mark was decoded — a measure of the surviving watermark, not of authenticity: the proof of what the capture looked like is the verified original itself.
